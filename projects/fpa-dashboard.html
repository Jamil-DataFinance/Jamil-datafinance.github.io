01The problem

Management needs answers, not more sales charts

A finance director wants to know four things every month: are we on budget, which products and channels make money after all costs, which customers are slow to pay, and what happens to EBITDA if growth slows or costs rise? The answers usually sit in separate spreadsheets owned by different people, so the monthly review spends its time reconciling instead of deciding.

02My approach

Six pages, each built around one decision

  1. Measures before visualsActual, budget and prior year with variances in value and %, written and tested in DAX first so every page agrees.
  2. Bridges, not just totalsA Revenue-to-EBITDA bridge shows exactly where gross revenue goes: discounts, refunds, cost of sales, marketing, overheads and shipping.
  3. Profit by product, not just revenueA full product P&L, because the biggest sellers are not always the most profitable.
  4. Working capital in the same packAn ageing page laid out the way a credit-control meeting runs: who owes what, how late, how risky.
  5. Scenarios inside the reportSliders recalculate the P&L live, so leadership can test an assumption in the meeting.
03How it helps the decision

From "what happened" to "what we do next"

Budget

Fix the categories that missed, not the whole business

Grocery is 2.6m and Beauty 0.9m below budget while Electronics is far ahead. Targeted action instead of a blanket push.

Evidence: Sales analysis page
Margin

Watch the mix

Electronics is half of revenue but earns the lowest gross margin (25%). Growth there grows revenue faster than profit.

Evidence: Sales and Profitability pages
Cash

Chase the high-risk receivables first

2.6m sits in the high-risk band, led by MegaMart Holdings and GlobMart Inc (over 50% risk scores).

Evidence: Customer ageing page
Planning

Test assumptions before committing

Leadership can see what +25% growth and tighter cost control do to EBITDA in the meeting, instead of waiting a week for a reforecast.

Evidence: What-if page
