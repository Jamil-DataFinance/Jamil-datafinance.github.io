01The problem

A plan that looks good in profit can still run out of cash

Boards and lenders ask the same questions: how fast can the business grow, what will it earn, and will it generate enough cash to fund itself and repay its debt? A forecast built statement by statement can't answer that, because growth, working capital, capex and funding all move together.

02My approach

Assumptions in, three linked statements out

  1. Actuals set the baselineThree historical years anchor every forecast assumption.
  2. Fully linked statementsProfit flows to retained earnings and the cash flow drives closing cash, so the balance sheet balances by construction.
  3. Supporting schedulesDepreciation, debt and working capital drive the forecast instead of hard-coded numbers.
  4. Inputs kept separateAssumptions such as growth and inflation sit apart from calculations, so scenarios and sensitivities run in seconds.
  5. One page for the boardAn executive dashboard summarises five years of forecast and the valuation.
